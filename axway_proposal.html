Axway uses Intercom today — but primarily as an L1 routing layer, not as a strategic support asset. Customers want AI-driven, self-service answers for quick questions, and the team hasn't yet unlocked that value.

Axway's technical support team is deeply skilled — but they're spending cycles on questions AI could answer instantly. Freeing them from repeatable L1 queries unlocks their true value: complex, high-impact technical engagement.

How to Evaluate an AI Agent

Six dimensions of effective evaluation
Most AI evaluations fail — not because the technology doesn't work, but because it's evaluated incorrectly. Teams focus on deflection instead of resolution, and demos instead of real usage.
1
Most Important

Resolution Quality

Whether customers actually get what they need. Answer quality is what drives trust and satisfaction.

2

Real-World Complexity

Can it handle multi-step workflows, edge cases, and variation in how customers ask questions? This is where most solutions fall short.

3

Accuracy at Scale

Consistency across thousands of interactions — not just isolated examples. Critical for a platform like Axway serving enterprise customers across integration, API management, and MFT.

4

Speed

Instant responses without sacrificing quality. Speed alone is not sufficient — responses must be both fast and correct.

5

Safety & Trust

Incorrect answers in billing or account configuration have real consequences. The system must know when to escalate.

6

Continuous Improvement

AI is not static. Evaluate how quickly gaps can be identified and addressed. Long-term value comes from compounding improvement.

Common pitfalls to avoid

  • Over-indexing on demos vs. real production data
  • Focusing only on deflection, not resolution quality
  • Testing too narrowly — simple scenarios only
  • Ignoring how performance improves over time

Knowledge Readiness Audit

Is your knowledge AI-ready?

AI performance is directly tied to the quality of the knowledge it can access. Run this practical audit before scaling.

1

Identify highest-volume conversations. Pull recent support data and group by topic. Focus on the top drivers of volume — these have the biggest impact on AI performance.

2

Map content coverage. For each high-volume category, check whether you have clear, customer-facing content that answers the question end-to-end. Note any gaps.

3

Review accuracy and freshness. Sample your most-used articles and validate them against the current product experience. Flag anything outdated or inconsistent.

4

Evaluate structure and clarity. Strong articles answer a single question and are easy to scan. Identify content that's too long, unstructured, or written for internal audiences.

5

Confirm a single source of truth. If answers are spread across multiple tools or rely on tribal knowledge, consolidate into one reliable system the AI can consistently reference.

6

Test content against real conversations. Take actual customer questions and see how well your content answers them — this surfaces gaps and edge cases.

7

Evaluate your maintenance process. Ensure a clear process exists for incorporating product changes and continuously improving content as the product evolves.

Pilot Approach

Pilot → Proof → Scale
1

Define scope

Select high-volume, repeatable questions. Establish clear success metrics: resolution rate, CSAT, efficiency gains.

2

Setup and training

Connect Fin to knowledge sources. Configure initial workflows and prepare content for accuracy and coverage.

3

Pilot launch (2–4 weeks)

Deploy to a subset of customers. Monitor in real time. Collect both quantitative metrics and qualitative feedback.

4

Evaluate and optimize

Review against success criteria. Identify knowledge gaps. Improve accuracy and expand use cases.

5

Scale

Expand to additional products or regions. Increase coverage. Integrate more deeply into workflows.

Starting with a targeted pilot ensures decisions about scaling are based on measured results, not assumptions.
Why this approach works
  • Validates impact quickly using real data
  • Quantifies ROI and efficiency gains before committing to scale
  • Builds internal alignment based on evidence
  • Keeps risk low while maximizing learning
